When a production line hits a throughput bottleneck, the first question is
usually not "should we automate," but "should we upgrade one station, or
redesign the whole line." The two paths differ significantly in investment
scale, downtime risk, and implementation time, so it's worth clarifying a
few decision factors first.

## Is the Bottleneck Concentrated at a Single Station?

If takt time analysis shows the bottleneck is clearly concentrated at one or
two stations, with capacity to spare elsewhere, a targeted upgrade is
usually the higher-value choice: automation is introduced only at the
bottleneck station, the rest of the line and existing flow stay unchanged,
and both implementation cost and downtime risk stay lower.

Conversely, if multiple stations are all near capacity, upgrading just one
may simply shift the bottleneck to the next station. That situation usually
calls for more comprehensive line planning.

## Remaining Useful Life and Interface Compatibility of Existing Equipment

If existing equipment is still within its useful service life and its
signal interface can connect to new modules, a targeted upgrade preserves
that existing investment. If existing equipment is old enough that its
signal interface is difficult to make compatible, or maintenance costs are
already high, folding it into a full rebuild plan is often more practical.

## Will the Product Line Keep Changing?

If the product line is expected to change meaningfully in the foreseeable
future (new variants, new processes), a full line design should build in
flexibility from the start, rather than doing a targeted upgrade now and
needing major adjustment later. Conversely, if product plans are relatively
stable, the return on a targeted upgrade addressing the current bottleneck
is more predictable.

## Acceptable Downtime

A targeted upgrade can usually be phased in with less impact on daily
production; a full rebuild typically requires a longer shutdown or a period
running parallel lines. How much downtime is actually acceptable directly
determines which approach is feasible on a given timeline.

## Summary

A targeted upgrade and a full rebuild aren't mutually exclusive. In most
cases the real path is phased: address the clearest bottleneck first, while
keeping room in the plan for future expansion. The actual decision still
requires evaluating on-site takt time data, equipment condition, and product
plans item by item; there's no single formula that applies to every line.
