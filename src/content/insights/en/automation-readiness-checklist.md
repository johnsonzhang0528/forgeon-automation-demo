Before an engineering team can quote or propose a plan, they typically need a
baseline set of information. Incomplete information doesn't mean a
conversation can't start, but how complete it is directly affects how
accurate the proposal will be and how likely it is to change later. Below are
the items most commonly requested.

## Workpiece Drawings and Tolerance Range

Whether the station is for positioning, assembly, or inspection, the actual
dimensional drawing and tolerance range of the workpiece is the most basic
input. Tolerance range determines whether a vision positioning system is
needed, or whether a fixed mechanical fixture is sufficient.

If a formal drawing isn't available yet, physical measurements or photos can
give the engineering team a preliminary read, but a formal quotation still
requires drawing confirmation.

## Current Line Takt Time

Takt time determines how long the equipment has to complete one cycle. This
number affects the speed profile design of motion modules, the time budget
available for camera capture and processing, and whether multiple stations
need to run in parallel.

Without takt time data, the engineering team can work backward from a
throughput target, but measured data is generally more reliable than
theoretical figures.

## Existing Equipment Interfaces

For a localized automation upgrade rather than a brand-new line, the
mechanical interface, electrical signal type, and control system version of
existing equipment need to be confirmed first. This determines whether new
modules can connect directly, or whether additional signal conversion and
mechanical adjustment will be required.

## On-Site Space and Environmental Conditions

Available floor space, lighting conditions (which affect vision system
selection), and dust or oil contamination levels (which affect protection
rating selection) are all conditions that must be confirmed before actual
installation, to avoid designing a solution that can't be installed or that
runs unreliably.

## Quality Standards and Inspection Criteria

If a station involves inspection, clear pass/fail criteria are needed. The
clearer the standard, the more grounded the development of recognition logic
and decision parameters can be; when standards are vague, more prototyping
and test iteration is needed upfront.

## Summary

None of the above needs to be fully assembled at first contact. Most
projects start with a preliminary discussion and fill in details gradually
during solution evaluation. Preparing this information early lets the
conversation focus faster on actual technical feasibility, rather than
repeatedly confirming baseline conditions.
